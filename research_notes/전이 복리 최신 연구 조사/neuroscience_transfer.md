## Key Question 1: What exactly is factorized in the brain into "structure" versus "content", and how is the structure part reused when content is entirely new?

### Takeaway
Across the 2026 hippocampal-entorhinal literature, the factorized "structure" part is consistently a *content-independent metric/transition scaffold* (grid-like path-integration code, or a latent transition variable inferred from the *difference* between consecutive structural states), and "content" is the sensory/episodic identity bound to that scaffold by fast one-shot (Hebbian / pseudoinverse) learning. Reuse works because the scaffold is pre-configured or learned once and only the binding is relearned; models that bind content and structure in a single latent (unified latent space, VQ-VAE-only, vanilla RNN) fail or transfer less. Nothing in 2026 shows a *statistical prior over the form of explanations* being carried; what is carried is a *difference operator over structural states* plus an action policy that reads only that difference.

### Cited Findings

**Line (1): hippocampal-entorhinal structural codes**

- **Hwang, Neupane, Jazayeri, Fiete — "Structural generalization and continual learning enabled by factorized entorhinal-hippocampal memory and entorhinal-parietal action circuits", bioRxiv, DOI 10.64898/2026.08.25.747129, posted ~30 Aug 2026 (date inferred from figure paths; page does not print a date).** Humans (n=13 for the stats shown), monkeys, RNNs and a "Vector-HaSH-augmented Action policy" (VHA) were compared on an abstract image-sequence navigation task (start image, target image, joystick deflection, release at target; visual nav, mental nav with images hidden, novel start-target routes, new image sequences as new "environments"). Results: humans' first mental-nav epoch beat first visual-nav epoch (t(12) = −4.83, p = 0.0004); familiar vs novel routes did not differ (t(12) = 0.60, p = 0.56); humans learned later environments faster with higher asymptotes and did not lose environment 1. ### Inferences
- In every 2026 model that transfers, the *shared* object is a difference/transition operator over an abstract state space, and the *per-world* object is a binding from abstract states (or roles) to concrete content, learned fast (one-shot Hebbian, pseudoinverse, or an online grounding likelihood with a change-point rule). This matches the user's finding that relational action forms overlap (17/37 same full form) but a *statistical prior over explanation size/cell kind* did not help: the brain-inspired models never carry priors over surface form; they carry a graph and relearn the grounding.
- Ablations converge on the same failure mode the user hit: a single latent that mixes content and structure (unified latent, VQ-VAE-only, vanilla RNN) transfers poorly or not at all (reuse ratio 2.05 vs 3.20; RNN relearns each environment from scratch).
- Kimmel et al. is the only human study here with a *decaying* transfer term: schema transfer peaks in the second block of a new world and then vanishes. A practical rule: use the previous world's structure as a regularizer only for the first k observations, then drop it.

### Gaps
- No 2026 TEM update by Whittington/Behrens found (searched explicitly).
- Hwang et al. and Wang/Johnston/Fusi print most transfer effect sizes only in figures; full texts beyond 100k characters not read.
- "Abstract representation in the hippocampus predicts spontaneously adopted structure-based behavioral strategy" (bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.09.20.752934, Sept 2026, 48-page PDF) could not be read: PDF binary only and HTML page returned HTTP 429 twice. Title only; treat as unverified.
- Zhang et al. (2605.15733): no single dedicated "transfer gain" statistic; "zero-shot transfer" is supported by decoding accuracy and reuse ratio, not a learning-speed curve.

## Key Question 2: What does schema-consistent fast learning require (how many prior exemplars, what overlap), per 2026 evidence?

### Takeaway
The 2026 rodent evidence keeps the Tse-Morris recipe: ~6 prior associations of the *same relational form* (cue→place), acquired over weeks, after which 3–6 new associations of that form are learned within a single day, and this speed-up depends causally on hippocampal ripples during post-learning rest; a persistent OFC representation of the *old* schema predicts *faster* learning of a *conflicting* one. The 2026 human work adds that schema transfer requires the *same structural form* (same latent-context transition structure with new content) and that its benefit is front-loaded and then fades. No 2026 study established a formal minimum number of exemplars.

### Cited Findings

**Line (2): schema theory and consolidation**

- **Bhattarai & Dragoi — "Offline generative network reconfiguration guides insight-like accelerated learning by assimilation into schema in rats", Nature Communications 17, online 3 Sept 2026 (PMID 42722686, PMCID PMC13562695).** Male rats learned six cue-place pairings over several weeks (the schema); afterwards they acquired 3 to 6 new pairings within a single day. Simultaneous HPC and mPFC recordings across exploration, rest and sleep: fast learning combined inferred activation of map-based cue-place associations with offline reconfiguration of the hippocampal network, coordinated with generalized outcome coding in mPFC; HPC reconfiguration during post-learning sleep/rest *predicted* accelerated learning; new pairings transferred to mPFC rapidly via ripple-coordinated co-activation; disrupting hippocampal ripples during post-learning rest *prevented* schema-based accelerated learning. - **Maor, Atwell, Ascher, Zhao, Takahashi, Hart, Pereira, Schoenbaum — "Persistent representation of a prior schema in the orbitofrontal cortex facilitates learning of a conflicting schema", Nature Communications 17:2610, online 10 Feb 2026 (PMID 41667512, PMCID PMC13003079).** Rats, odor problems with identical external information but different reward rules (conflicting schemas); OFC single units + temporary inactivation. Authors: OFC aids learning by keeping an accurate representation of the old schema *alongside* the new, not by interference. No numbers in abstract. — [Nature Comms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-69330-2), [NIDA summary](https://irp.nida.nih.gov/hotp-april-16-2026/), [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1038/s41467-026-69330-2&format=json&resultType=core). **[MECH-PARTIAL]**: do *not* overwrite or average the old world's model into a prior; keep it intact as a separate, contrastable hypothesis; the speed-up comes from having something explicit to contrast against.

- **Wang Ruimin — "Beyond Temporary Scaffolding: Persistent Hippocampal Engagement and Brain-Wide Network Reorganization in Remote Memory Consolidation", bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.07.26.740756, 28 Jul 2026 (38 pp).** Search snippet reports a two-phase account (schema-mediated then semantic-mediated) with hub shifting from vmPFC toward medial prefrontal and parietal regions. PDF content could not be extracted (binary streams); treat as title + search-snippet only. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2026/07/28/2026.07.26.740756.full.pdf). **[MECH-NO]** (unverified).

- [PRECURSOR] **Tse et al., Science 2007 (PMID 17412951).** Six flavour-place paired associates form the schema; thereafter a new pair is learned in a single trial and becomes hippocampus-independent within ~48 h; later work: 2 new pairs consistent with the schema were absorbed, 6 pairs designed to be inconsistent were not. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17412951/) (abstract-level confirmation via search; no threshold below six exemplars has been established in any source read).

- [PRECURSOR] **Kumaran, Hassabis, McClelland — "What learning systems do intelligent agents need? ### Inferences
- "Schema-consistent" in 2026 rodent work means *the new item instantiates the same relational template* (cue→place; odor→rule) that ≥6 prior items instantiated; the content is new. That is exactly the user's situation (shared action forms, new objects/attributes), so the brain's answer is not "carry a prior over explanation form" but "carry the template and an index that tells where the new item slots in".
- Both rat papers put the mechanism *offline* (ripple-coordinated reconfiguration; persistent old representation). An implementable analogue: after each world, run a consolidation pass that rewrites stored explanations into a shared role-indexed form, and keep old-world models as explicit alternatives.
- Benefit is front-loaded: Kimmel et al. show schema transfer contributing mainly in block 2; by later blocks local learning dominates. A transfer test at large k (k=25) will therefore look like NONE even when transfer is real; test at the first few observations.

### Gaps
- No 2026 paper gives a formal threshold for number of prior exemplars or a quantitative "overlap" measure; six remains an empirical convention from Tse et al.
- No 2026 human schema study with trial-level speed-up numbers was found (searched "schema consolidation 2026", "van Kesteren SLIMM 2026", "Gilboa Marlatte 2026" implicitly via general queries; nothing new surfaced). SLIMM and Gilboa/Marlatte had no 2026 updates in the results returned.

## Key Question 3: Is there a 2026 computational model that gets faster at genuinely novel task families through structural codes, and by how much?

### Takeaway
Yes, three, but each on a *single* structural family with new content, not on genuinely new families: VHA (Hwang/Fiete) learns ~3× faster than an RNN and is near-ceiling from trial 1 in a new environment; S-HAI (Van de Maele/Pezzulo) reaches near-optimal reward in 39 novel blocks from one block of schema learning, matching an agent trained on all 40; the HPC-MEC world model (Zhang/Wu) raises OOD transition decoding from 0.64–0.85 (baselines/ablations) to 0.91 and reuse ratio from ~2.0–2.4 to 3.2. The human gSR data (Kimmel) show zero-shot above-chance inference on entirely new stimuli by the third session. None tests transfer across *different* relational forms; when content dominates (Push-T, large distribution shift) the gains shrink.

**Line (3): prefrontal abstract rules and latent states**

## Key Question 4: Which findings argue AGAINST transfer (interference, schema-induced false memories, far-transfer meta-analysis)?

### Takeaway
The "2026 far-transfer meta-analysis g = 0.02" does not exist as a 2026 publication: g = 0.02 (SE 0.06, k = 16, p = .717, 95% CI [−0.10, 0.15]) is from Sala, Tatlidil, Tatsumi, Gondo & Gobet's second-order meta-analysis (Collabra, 2019) of working-memory training; all 2026-dated hits were navigation artifacts. The real 2026 negatives are: schema bias in recall rises when memory precision falls (n = 423, Cognition 2026); related memories interfere during learning in adults but at retrieval in children (PLoS One 2026); prefrontal rule units fragment into separate per-task populations without thalamic stabilization (Science Advances 2026); and models that keep a unified latent lose transfer (arXiv 2026). The strongest 2026 counter-example to "prior knowledge interferes" is the OFC finding that a persistent old schema *speeds* learning a conflicting one.

### Cited Findings

- **Sala, Tatlidil, Tatsumi, Gondo, Gobet — "Near and Far Transfer in Cognitive Training: A Second-Order Meta-Analysis", Collabra: Psychology 5(1):18, 2019.** Far-transfer model: g = 0.02 (SE = 0.06, k = 16, p = .717), 95% CI [−0.10; 0.15]; corrected ≈ 0.01; zero heterogeneity; omnibus with active controls g = 0.03 (k = 70). Authors: WM-training far-transfer impact is null regardless of population. — [Collabra](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/5/1/18/113004/Near-and-Far-Transfer-in-Cognitive-Training-A). **[PRECURSOR]**. No 2026 far-transfer meta-analysis was found; related children's EF meta-analyses give far transfer g = 0.11 (k = 17, p = .11) and g = 0.12. — [PubMed 30652908](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30652908/). **[MECH-NO]**: null far transfer in humans when the trained and test tasks share no explicit structure; consistent with the brain-model view that transfer needs an identical relational template.

- **Ramey — "Prior knowledge biases episodic memory by filling in the gaps in imprecise memories: Simulating aging effects in younger adults", Cognition 274:106565, online 12 May 2026 (PMID 42119328).** Two studies, n = 423; scenes shown 3× or 1×; object locations schema-consistent or -inconsistent. - **Woodbury, Duan, Brol, Seeger, Yang, Schlichting — "Related memories exhibit interference during learning in adults but later retrieval in children", PLoS One 21(9):e0356464, 11 Sept 2026.** Children 4–16 and young adults learned object locations that could or could not be added to previously learned 2-D maps, same day. - **Zhang et al. arXiv 2605.15733 ablations (see KQ1):** unified latent space reuse ratio 2.054 vs 3.201; VQ-VAE ablation retains "excessive source information, yielding inferior transfer". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15733). **[MECH-YES]**: negative result that names the failure: carrying content-laden statistics.

- **Renz et al. (see KQ3):** structure-learning measures did *not* predict replay; only value updates did; MTL structure alignment collapsed to n.s. after a covert structure change. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.12.693963v2.full). **[MECH-PARTIAL]**: argues against expecting structure itself to be what fast processes carry.

- **Counter-evidence to interference:** Maor et al., Nature Comms Feb 2026 (see KQ2): persistent old-schema representation in OFC predicted *faster* learning of a conflicting schema; OFC disruption during schema 1 *impaired* schema 2. — [Nature Comms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-69330-2).

### Inferences
- The anti-transfer literature and the pro-transfer literature agree on the boundary: transfer appears only when the new task instantiates the *same relational template*, and fails (or hurts) when what is carried is content-correlated statistics (schema fill-in under low precision; unified latents). The user's negative result (priors over explanation size/cell kind, per-outcome-kind, whole-action pattern with input roles) sits on the "content-correlated statistics" side even though it was phrased over structure.
- The developmental interference result and the Ramey result together predict that any carried prior will show its *cost* at low k (sparse evidence) and its *benefit* only if it is an exact template; this matches the pattern "life beats none only at k=3 on desk and view, wrong rises on clime and dye at low k".

### Gaps
- No 2026 meta-analysis of far transfer exists in the sources found; the g = 0.02 figure is 2019.
- No 2026 schema-induced *false memory* study beyond Ramey was found; "SLIMM 2026" and "Gilboa Marlatte 2026" returned nothing new.

## Summary for the symbolic learner (synthesis across lines)

1. **What the brain carries:** a content-free transition/difference operator over an abstract state space (grid Δ, Δg→z, rank-1 SR structure, A→B→C→D graph) and a policy/update rule defined over that difference; plus, after consolidation, intact old-world models kept as explicit alternatives (OFC). It does not carry statistics over the *form* of explanations.
2. **What it rebuilds from scratch:** the binding of abstract slots/roles to concrete content (one-shot Hebbian; pseudoinverse; online grounding likelihood with a change-point threshold), and the per-world value/effect estimates.
3. **When fast assimilation happens:** when ≥~6 prior items instantiated the *same* relational template; the gain is front-loaded (first 1–3 observations), depends on an *offline* reconfiguration pass, and requires that the *same* rule units be reused rather than new ones minted per world.
4. **Why the user's attempts failed, per these mechanisms:** priors over explanation size/cell kind and per-outcome statistics are content-correlated summaries (the "unified latent"/"schema fill-in" failure modes), which the literature shows degrade or do not help transfer and raise errors under sparse evidence.
5. **Implementable candidates (ranked by evidence):** (a) fixed role-indexed slot structure for actions with content bound per world and a Δ-predictor shared across worlds [Hwang; Zhang; Van de Maele]; (b) previous-world structure used as a *decaying* regularizer only for the first few observations [Kimmel]; (c) an offline re-indexing pass after each world that rewrites stored explanations into shared role form and keeps old-world models intact for contrast [Bhattarai & Dragoi; Maor]; (d) transitive linkage via shared concrete elements across worlds [Townsend]; (e) diagnostic: measure whether the same explanation nodes are reused across worlds (thalamic-stability analogue) and the parallelism/CCGP of effect representations [Wang Z; Wang/Johnston/Fusi].
