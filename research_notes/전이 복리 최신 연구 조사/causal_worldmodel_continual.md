# Causal representation learning, object-centric world models, continual forward transfer, representation birth, successor-feature/schema transfer (June–Nov 2026, with precursors)

Scope note. Searched arXiv (cs.LG/cs.AI/stat.ML), PMLR (CLeaR 2026 = vol. 323; CoLLAs), NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR 2026 mentions. Every paper below was opened (arXiv abstract page and/or HTML full text, or PMLR page). Dates are the arXiv submission dates on the page. "PRECURSOR" marks anything before June 2026. Where a page gave no numbers I say so rather than infer any.

The target system for the judgement column: a symbolic per-action effect predictor (Bayesian averaging over small explanation sets, six tool-call text worlds with objects and attributes, no LLM at runtime) whose three attempts to carry explanation-form priors across worlds did not beat an empty mind.

## Key question 1: Which 2026 results show positive forward transfer to a genuinely different environment family, and how large?

### Takeaway
No June–Nov 2026 paper I could verify shows large positive forward transfer to a *genuinely different* environment family; the positive numbers all sit inside one family (same game with a related rule, same gridworld with changed slip/reward, same task stream). The largest verified in-family gains are ~5x fewer moves-to-convergence between two related hidden rules (object-centric agent) and ~2x label efficiency from replay selection in LLM continual learning; cross-family transfer is reported as zero or negative in the same papers.

### Cited Findings
- **Game of Hidden Rules (Rutgers/USC), arXiv 2608.21372, submitted 26 June 2026, no venue.** Transformer A2C agent, 6x6 board, 9 objects with shape+color, accept/deny/immovable feedback, reward 0/−1; hidden rule never shown. Transfer metric T = moves-to-convergence(transfer)/moves-to-convergence(scratch); T<1 positive. Object-centric (OC) representation: ordL1↔quadNearby both reach T≈0.2 (≈5x speedup); cm_RBKY↔cw_0123 ≈0.5. Feature-centric (FC): pretraining on cm_RBKY alone gives ≈0.13 on the compound target but FC stays ≈1 on most other curricula. Positive transfer is "concentrated primarily within related rule families" (bucket-ordering family; feature-to-bucket family). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21372); [HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21372)
- Same paper, negative side: for OC, cw_0123 alone gives T>1.7; ordL1→cw_0123 and quadNearby→cw_0123 give T>2 and >3; several unrelated-only curricula exceed 3. "Unrelated pretraining generally produces negative transfer"; penalty larger for OC than FC. Row/column clustering of the transfer matrix is coherent in OC (ARI 0.633, Baker's gamma 0.542) and noisy in FC (0.168, 0.039). — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21372)
- **Rethinking Transfer in Continual Learning: A Replay-Based Realisation (U. Chicago), arXiv 2607.15587, 17 July 2026, preprint.** Defines forward transfer as the contribution of prior experience under a fixed target-data budget; positive only when (headroom on target) AND (a persistent carrier) AND (the right source). LLM streams TRACE-8 and NumGLUE-8, Qwen2.5-0.5B/7B with one LoRA, 50 examples/task, 5 seeds. Transfer-selective replay: 0.439 vs 0.427 (uniform replay) plasticity on TRACE-8; 0.353 vs 0.336 accuracy on NumGLUE-8; probe gain ≈+0.066 at N=10 ≈ "2x label efficiency". Harmful source–target cells up to +0.27/+0.26 loss change. At 7B the best-source gain "drops to zero"; on PP15, 10x more target data adds only ≈+0.02 and parameter-reuse methods fall within one seed SD of baseline. Notes that forward transfer on modern LLM benchmarks is "routinely reported as absent or negative" and cites Choi & Kim 2026 (MERIT) and Chen et al. 2026 (OPR) as documenting negative transfer. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15587v1)
- **Robust Successor Features, arXiv 2609.31016, v1 25 Sep 2026, v2 4 Nov 2026, "to be published in EWRL 2026".** Successor features generalized over both reward w and transition kernel p in linear MDPs; GPI bound with a dynamics-mismatch term κ_pq = min(2, L_φ‖p−q‖) that collapses to the reward-only bound when q=p. Single stochastic 3x4 gridworld; train on p∈{0.65,0.95}, r∈{−0.02,−0.2}; evaluate over p∈[0.65,0.95], r∈[−0.20,−0.01]. Q-value MAE 0.063±0.025 (RSF) vs 0.131±0.069 (USFA); policy accuracy 93.6%±7.1 vs 86.7%±7.8. Reward features φ and dynamics features are *given*; learning them is future work. No returns, no robust-RL baseline, no new environment family. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31016); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31016)
- **Before We Adapt: The Zero-Shot Generalization Bottleneck in Continual World Models, arXiv 2610.41041, 29 Oct 2026, NeurIPS 2026 Continual World Models workshop.** FourRooms; Dyna-Q and TD-MPC trained on room00, frozen, tested on room10/room01/room11 and controlled translations (26, 39 cells) and layout changes. Zero-shot reuse "can degrade substantially under physical-context changes"; one-step prediction-error gap grows with distance from training range; position+direction observations are least robust, distance-to-obstacle observations "substantially more stable", combined intermediate; TD-MPC's learned latent does not remove the dependence. Only log10 error heatmaps, no returns. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41041); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.41041v1)
- **Investigating Knowledge Transfer Across Interactive Dialogue Games, arXiv 2608.23969, 25 Aug 2026, preprint (cs.CL).** LLMs fine-tuned on clembench games; transferability graph via binary-integer program (Zamir et al. 2018) plus task-vector similarity. Some games benefit more from another game than from themselves; the visuospatial family (exploration games) transfers best; task-vector similarity "reveals almost no transferability patterns". No numbers on the abstract page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23969)
- **PRECURSOR. Principled Fast and Meta Knowledge Learners (FAME), arXiv 2603.00903, 1 Mar 2026, ICLR 2026.** FT_i = (AUC_i − AUC_i^reset)/(1 − AUC_i^reset), Reset = 0. Meta-World (3 random sequences, 10 seeds): Reset 0.000; Finetune −0.265±0.028; Average −0.530±0.024; PackNet −0.194±0.018; FAME-KL 0.022±0.015; FAME-WD −0.003±0.014. Atari FT: Freeway Finetune 0.53 / FAME 0.68; SpaceInvaders 0.65 / 0.63. MinAtar FT: Finetune 0.13±0.03, LargeBuffer 0.16±0.02, FAME 0.16±0.03, MultiHead −0.01. All within one benchmark family. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00903); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00903)
- **PRECURSOR. Predictive Representations for Skill Transfer in RL, arXiv 2604.07016, 8 Apr 2026, preprint; comments say the work dates from 2018–2021.** Outcome-Predictive State Representations and OPSR-based skills learned from demonstrations speed learning on new tasks "considerably"; no numbers on the page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07016)
- **PRECURSOR. Continual Knowledge Adaptation for RL (CKA-RL), arXiv 2510.19314 (2025).** Reports forward transfer 0.7749 vs 0.6864 for fine-tuning (search snippet; in-family benchmark). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19314v2)
- **CoLLAs 2026 (5th, Bucharest, 14–17/18 Sep 2026):** proceedings not yet on PMLR as of 9 Nov 2026. PMLR vol. 330 (published 6 Apr 2026) is the *2025* CoLLAs. So no CoLLAs-2026 paper could be cited. — [PMLR v330](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v330/); [CoLLAs 2026 CFP](https://groups.google.com/g/ml-news/c/fP11vk8F7Dc)

### Inferences
- The one 2026 paper closest to the target system (hidden-rule induction over objects with attributes, accept/deny feedback) finds exactly the pattern the target system found: carrying knowledge across *incompatible relational structure* costs 2–3x more experience than starting empty, and the cost is *larger* for the object-centric representation because it transfers more coherently in both directions. That is a published replication of "life worse than none" across rule families, albeit with a neural A2C learner rather than a Bayesian symbolic one.
- Every positive 2026 transfer number comes with a stated structural overlap (same rule family, same linear-MDP feature map, same game interface). None of the verified results transfers a prior across environment families with disjoint action/attribute vocabularies. The 2607.15587 three-condition account (headroom, carrier, right source) is the only general explanation offered; the "right source" condition is the one the target system cannot satisfy when the next world's forms are unknown.
- Robust SF is the only 2026 SF-style result; its gain exists only because φ and the dynamics features are hand-given and shared. It says nothing about discovering the features, which is the target system's actual bottleneck.

### Gaps
- No CoLLAs 2026 or NeurIPS 2026 main-track paper with cross-family forward-transfer numbers could be located (NeurIPS 2026 accepted-paper lists exist but no causal-transfer paper surfaced in searches; CoLLAs 2026 proceedings unpublished).
- 2610.41041 and 2608.23969 give no numeric effect sizes on their abstract/HTML pages; 2604.07016 gives none.
- Could not find any 2026 paper measuring a sample-efficiency ratio vs from-scratch for a *symbolic/Bayesian* learner across text worlds; the closest is the Hidden Rules T-ratio for a neural learner.

## Key question 2: Any 2026 result on discovering variables/attributes from raw observations via intervention (not given factor labels)?

### Takeaway
Yes, but partial: the 2026 work that uses the agent's own actions as interventions discovers *which observation dimensions are controllable* (a binary mask), or orients *edges among already-named variables*; nothing verified in 2026 discovers the attribute vocabulary itself (which tokens form a mutually exclusive attribute) from raw text or pixels with no given semantics. Identifiability theory has moved to discrete latents and to compositional "concept modulation", still from given attribute/environment indices.

### Cited Findings
- **Intervene, Calibrate, Discover: Learning Causal Structure from Pixels, arXiv 2610.34711, 26 Oct 2026, preprint (cs.AI, stat.AP).** Setting: "node semantics are known but the graph is not". Fixed round-robin interventions provide verified values; a frozen encoder/decoder plus an invertible per-dimension calibration map align visual readouts to intervention units; the agent then chooses interventions to orient edges, which "equal amounts of additional passive observation cannot do". Benchmarks: Domino, Pick-and-Place, Balance, Gears, CAUSAL3D, CausalWorld. Reuses the authors' "four-concept model with four dimensions per concept" and label scales, so not label-free. No SHD/F1 numbers on the pages. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34711); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34711)
- **PRECURSOR (v3 in window). Discovering What You Can Control: Interventional Boundary Discovery (IBD), arXiv 2603.18257, v1 18 Mar 2026, v3 27 Sep 2026, no venue.** Treats the agent's own randomized actions as interventions; per-dimension two-sample tests with FDR correction yield a binary mask over observation dimensions. 12 continuous-control settings with up to 100 distractors; IBD matches oracle return in 11/12; observational baselines (MI, state-conditioned forward models, gradient sensitivity) often underperform passing the full observation to SAC; gradient attribution F1 only 0.44–0.50 on boundary discovery. Discovers controllability, not attribute identity. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18257)
- **PRECURSOR (CLeaR 2026, PMLR 323:1545–1571). Causal Discovery in Action: Learning Chain-Reaction Mechanisms from Interventions, arXiv 2603.22620, v1 23 Mar 2026, v2 3 Apr 2026.** Chain-reaction (Rube-Goldberg-like) systems: structure uniquely identifiable from *blocking* interventions on individual components; minimal estimator with finite-sample guarantees (exponential error decay, logarithmic sample complexity); recovery "from a few interventions" where observational heuristics fail under delayed/overlapping effects. Components are given; what is discovered is the wiring. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22620); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v323/panayiotou26a.html)
- **PRECURSOR (CLeaR 2026 Oral, PMLR 323). Disentangling Dynamical Systems: CRL Meets Local Sparse Attention, arXiv 2603.14483, v1 15 Mar 2026, v2 11 Jun 2026.** Graphical identifiability criterion for recovering disentangled system parameters from raw trajectories up to permutation and diffeomorphism; local (state-dependent) causal structure gives strictly stronger guarantees than global structure; sparsity-regularised transformer; four synthetic domains where baselines fail. Input is raw trajectories, no function library; no OOD/transfer numbers. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14483); [PMLR v323 index](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v323/)
- **PRECURSOR (CLeaR 2026, PMLR 323:1481–1525). Intervening to Learn and Compose Causally Disentangled Representations (Markham, Hirsch, Chang, Solus, Aragam), arXiv 2507.04754, v1 7 Jul 2025, v2 2 Apr 2026.** Adds a "context module" to a black-box generative model that "selectively modifies its architecture during training" (intervention-inspired); new identifiability result; compositions used for OOD generation on real and simulated data. Whether intervention targets are given is not stated on the page; no numbers on the page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04754); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v323/markham26a.html)
- **Discrete Causal Representations from Heterogeneous Domains: A Bayesian Approach (Garg, Stettler, Schein, von Kügelgen), arXiv 2606.06288, 4 Jun 2026, preprint.** Discrete latent causal concepts, unknown multi-node soft interventions across environments (countries/states), priors encoding the sparse-mechanism-shift assumption (a search snippet reports a prior expected sparsity of 90% for which mechanisms shift), sequential Monte Carlo over a multimodal posterior. Qualitative case studies only; no identifiability theorem claimed. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06288)
- **PRECURSOR. Discrete Causal Representation Learning (Zhang, Wang, Gu), arXiv 2603.25017, 26 Mar 2026, preprint (stat.ME).** Discrete latent DAG + sparse bipartite latent-to-observed graph; claims both graphs identifiable from the observational distribution "under mild conditions"; three-stage pipeline (penalized estimation, latent resampling, score-based discovery) with consistency. Educational assessment and synthetic image data; no numbers on page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25017)
- **Concept Modulation Models (Yi, Lu, Squires, Ravikumar), arXiv 2606.18509, 16 Jun 2026, preprint.** Attribute → modulator → concept → feature; "attribute potentials" (log-density ratios of attribute-conditioned concept distributions) give algebraic criteria for when agreement on observed attributes extends to unseen attributes (extrapolation); recovers several prior identifiability results as special cases. Purely theoretical; attributes are given indices. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18509)
- **PRECURSOR. Multi-Domain Empirical Bayes for Linearly-Mixed Causal Representations (Wu, von Kügelgen, Blei), arXiv 2603.18404, 19/21 Mar 2026.** Treats multi-domain data as interventions on a shared SCM; EB f-modeling via causally structured score matching; assumes linear mixing with *known* graph and intervention targets; better estimation than other CRL methods on synthetic data. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18404)
- **PRECURSOR. Causal-JEPA (Nam, Le Lidec, Maes, LeCun, Balestriero), arXiv 2602.11389, v1 11 Feb 2026, v2 28 May 2026, ICML 2026.** Masks object-level latents to create "counterfactual-like" prediction queries; ~20% absolute VQA gain over same architecture without object masking; Push-T control with 1.02% of the latent features of patch-based DINO-WM at comparable success. Object slots are assumed, not discovered; masking is a training signal, not variable discovery. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11389)
- **PRECURSOR. CausalVAE as a Plug-in for World Models, arXiv 2604.07712, 9 Apr 2026.** Adds a structural DAG branch to object-centric latent dynamics using "alignment-only weak supervision" to anchor coordinates; on Physics, CF-H@1 +102.5% averaged over 8 paired baselines, 11.0→41.0 in GNN-NLL. Weakly supervised. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07712)
- **Low-Rank Dynamics-Effective Latent Carriers for Counterfactual Rollout, arXiv 2608.15156, v1 15 Aug 2026, v4 7 Sep 2026, preprint (cs.RO).** Two-object collision world, 192-d recurrent model; a rank-4 hidden-state patch is the smallest on a preregistered grid that drives a 12-step autonomous counterfactual rollout; replicates on 2 of 3 fresh checkpoints; position-edit stress test fails specificity controls; authors explicitly deny this is "a closed four-dimensional state". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15156)
- **Compact but Moving: Intervention-Relevant Geometry in Recurrent World Models, arXiv 2609.21787, 18 Sep 2026, preprint.** The rank-4 correction leaves its entry subspace during rollout; intervention structure persists as "a moving, state-dependent local geometry" rather than a fixed latent variable; response reconfiguration in 2 of 3 checkpoints. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21787)

### Inferences
- The 2026 field splits variable discovery into (a) controllability masks from own actions (IBD), (b) wiring among given components (chain reactions, Intervene-Calibrate-Discover), and (c) identifiability of discrete latents from given environment/attribute indices. The target system's "representation birth" step (attributes as classes of tokens that replaced each other under own actions, with mutual exclusivity) sits in a gap none of these fill: it discovers the *attribute partition* itself. IBD's two-sample-test-per-dimension idea is the nearest transferable mechanism (replace "dimension" with "token slot"; replace "controllable" with "changed under this action").
- The two recurrent-world-model papers (2608.15156, 2609.21787) are a cautionary result for anyone hoping to read fixed causal variables out of a learned latent: the intervention-relevant directions move with state. A symbolic learner that names attributes explicitly avoids this problem by construction.
- Discrete-latent identifiability (2603.25017, 2606.06288) is the theory line most compatible with a symbolic predictor; both still take environment/domain labels as given and offer no finite-sample transfer guarantees.

### Gaps
- No 2026 paper found that discovers attribute vocabularies from raw *text* observations via interventions. Search for symbolic/relational rule learning across text games returned only the Hidden Rules board game and LLM dialogue-game transfer.
- Intervene-Calibrate-Discover gives no SHD/F1 on its pages; Markham et al. and Baumgartner et al. give no quantitative OOD numbers on abstract pages.
- Could not confirm whether Causal-JEPA's object slots come from a pretrained segmenter.

## Key question 3: Any 2026 theory (identifiability, sparse mechanism shift, ICM) that predicts when carrying a prior over mechanisms helps or hurts?

### Takeaway
One June 2026 theory paper gives a tight finite-sample answer: carrying causal-invariance knowledge helps only when the *target-risk margins* between candidate (invariant) predictors are large relative to target sample size (n_Q ≳ 1/Δ), and no algorithm can do better than target-only learning when margins are small; an adaptive aggregator is provably never worse than target-only (no negative transfer). Large structural shift ⇒ large margin ⇒ transfer helps; small shift ⇒ nothing to gain. The 2607.15587 three-condition account (headroom, carrier, right source) is the empirical counterpart.

### Cited Findings
- **How Useful is Causal Invariance for Domain Adaptation in Finite-Sample Settings? (Kostin, Jalaldoust, Bareinboim, Kpotufe, Yang), arXiv 2606.12680, 10 Jun 2026, preprint.** Linear regression; causal knowledge defines a set of (possibly) invariant feature subsets I, each a source-trained candidate predictor. Margin Δ_I = ε_I − ε_I*. Upper bound (Thm 3.1): with prob ≥1−α, excess target risk ≤ min{ C(d+log(1/α))/n_Q , ε_I* + Δ_max(n_P,n_Q) + 3r(n_P) } with r(n_P)=O(√(d/n_P)); the first term "guarantees no negative transfer" vs target-only. Sample complexity n_Q ≳ max{log|H|/inf_I Δ_I, C}. Lower bounds (Thm 3.2, 3.3): if n_Q ≤ c_0/Δ, any selector, and any algorithm in the two-model case, fails with constant probability — the 1/Δ dependence is tight. Section 4.2: "large structural shifts induce large target-risk margins between candidate models" in linear SCMs. Validated on Causal Chamber and gene-expression data (numbers not in the portion read). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12680); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12680)
- **Rethinking Transfer in Continual Learning, arXiv 2607.15587, 17 Jul 2026.** Transfer positive only with (i) headroom S_t(N_max) − S_t(N) > 0, (ii) a persistent carrier (replay persists; "one-shot parameter initialisation is washed out"), (iii) a compatible source; a gradient-based task signature predicted the best source for 4 of 8 targets, input-similarity and distributional signatures did worse. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15587v1)
- **Concept Modulation Models, arXiv 2606.18509, 16 Jun 2026.** Extrapolation to unseen attributes holds exactly when attribute-potential identities extend to those attributes — an algebraic criterion for "when does what I learned about seen attributes say anything about an unseen one". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18509)
- **Robust Successor Features, arXiv 2609.31016.** GPI bound: suboptimality ≤ (2/(1−γ))·[min_C(‖w−z‖ + κ_pq‖z‖/(1−γ)) + dynamics term in κ_pq‖w‖ + ρ_C], κ_pq = min(2, L_φ‖p−q‖). The bound quantifies exactly how much a carried policy library degrades as dynamics drift. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31016)
- **PRECURSOR. Disentangling Dynamical Systems (CLeaR 2026 Oral).** Global causal structure gives only a lower bound on what local, state-dependent structure can guarantee; enforcing locality is "often necessary for full identifiability". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14483)
- **PRECURSOR. Nonparametric Partial Disentanglement via Mechanism Sparsity (Lachapelle et al.), arXiv 2401.04890, revised 2026.** States that sparsity of the action-to-latent graph "corresponds precisely to the sparse mechanism shift hypothesis" of Schölkopf et al. 2021. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.04890)
- **PRECURSOR (2025). A Shift in Perspective on Causality in Domain Generalization, arXiv 2508.12798.** A shift "has to be large before causal modelling pays off" over a spurious predictor that performs well in-distribution. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12798)
- **PRECURSOR (CLeaR 2026 title only, PMLR 323).** "Sharp Bounds for Treatment Effect Generalization under Outcome Distribution Shift" (Asiaee et al.), "Bayesian Hierarchical Invariant Prediction" (Madaleno et al.), "Domain Faithfulness through Counterfactually Robust Learning" (Balashankar et al.) appear in the CLeaR 2026 index; not opened individually. — [PMLR v323](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v323/)
- **Bayesian transfer theory (older, for reference).** A Bayesian analysis of online transfer learning shows an improper prior can cause negative transfer while a proper prior avoids it when source and target differ. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370223001376)

### Inferences
- Applied to the target system: carrying an explanation-form prior is a "candidate predictor" in the Kostin et al. sense. It can help only if (a) the new world's mechanisms differ *enough* from an empty-prior predictor that the margin Δ is large, and (b) the carried candidate is actually the right one. When the carried form is wrong, their theory says the only safe thing is an *adaptive aggregator* that includes the target-only (empty-mind) predictor as a candidate and selects by target risk — which guarantees no negative transfer but also no gain until n_Q ≳ 1/Δ. The three NO-GO results (life = none at k=3..25) are consistent with the small-margin regime: on dev seeds, the empty mind already learns each world's few forms fast, leaving little headroom.
- The 2607.15587 "carrier" condition suggests a concrete reason the prior-adjustment route failed: a prior over explanation size or cell kind is a one-shot initialization, which that paper finds is washed out; replaying actual past cases (data, not a summary statistic) is the carrier that persisted. The target system's finding that the marginal never-met-value statistic adds nothing matches this.
- None of the 2026 theory predicts *positive* transfer of mechanism *form* across worlds with disjoint vocabularies; all results condition on shared features (φ), shared attribute indices, or shared causal variables.

### Gaps
- Kostin et al.'s empirical sections (Causal Chamber, gene expression) and the exact shift-to-margin bound of Section 4.2 were beyond the portion of the HTML that was read; no numbers reported here.
- No 2026 paper found that treats the *sparse mechanism shift hypothesis* as a testable prediction of when to carry priors; 2606.06288 only uses it as a prior (90% expected sparsity).
- No ICM-theoretic 2026 result found on transfer of mechanism *form* (as opposed to mechanism parameters).

## Key question 4: Negative results — where did lifelong learning show zero or negative forward transfer in 2026?

### Takeaway
Negative or zero forward transfer is now documented in 2026 in three independent settings: hidden-rule induction over objects (T up to >3x worse than scratch across rule families, worse for object-centric agents), LLM continual learning (harmful source-target cells, zero best-source gain at 7B, parameter-reuse within noise on saturated bases), and continual RL on Meta-World (fine-tuning −0.265, PackNet −0.194, averaging −0.530; a proposed method only reaches +0.022). Zero-shot world-model reuse across room layouts degrades substantially.

### Cited Findings
- Game of Hidden Rules: "Unrelated pretraining generally produces negative transfer"; both representations need more episodes than scratch; OC T>3 for several unrelated curricula; FC quadrant-based predecessors cause "severe degradation" on ordering target cw; some pretrained cw checkpoints transfer to cw itself *worse than scratch*. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21372)
- Rethinking Transfer in CL: harmful cells up to +0.27/+0.26 loss change on fragile targets (cstance, scienceqa, numglue-cm); at 7B best-source gain = 0; on PP15, 10x more target data gives ≈+0.02 and parameter-reuse methods are within one seed SD of baseline. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15587v1)
- FAME (ICLR 2026, Mar 2026 PRECURSOR): Meta-World FT Finetune −0.265±0.028, Average −0.530±0.024, PackNet −0.194±0.018, FAME-WD −0.003±0.014, FAME-KL +0.022±0.015; MinAtar MultiHead −0.01. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00903)
- Before We Adapt (Oct 2026): frozen world models reused zero-shot across FourRooms layouts show substantially increased one-step error, growing with translation distance; learned TD-MPC latents do not remove the dependence; position-based observations worst. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.41041v1)
- PRECURSOR (Nov 2025). When Object-Centric World Models Meet Policy Learning: From Pixels to Policies, and Where It Breaks, arXiv 2511.06136 (v2 11 Nov 2025). Unsupervised disentangled OC world model (DLPWM) reconstructs and predicts well incl. OOD visual variations, but policies on its latents underperform DreamerV3 due to "representation shift during multi-object interactions"; no numbers on page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06136)
- PRECURSOR (Feb 2026). Are Object-Centric Representations Better at Compositional Generalization? arXiv 2602.16689, 18 Feb 2026. VQA on CLEVRTex/Super-CLEVR/MOVi-C: OC beats dense (DINOv2/SigLIP2) only in harder compositional settings and low-data/low-compute regimes; dense catches up or surpasses with enough data and diversity. No numbers on the page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16689)
- Robust SF ablations: restricting the carried library to reward-only raises Q MAE from 0.063 to 0.186 (worse than the USFA baseline 0.131); a mis-specified carried library is worse than the plain baseline. — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31016)

### Inferences
- The Hidden Rules paper is the sharpest 2026 analogue to the target system's "life vs none vs shuffled" test: coherent (object-centric) knowledge transfers strongly when relational structure matches and hurts strongly when it does not; the diffuse (feature-centric) representation is closer to zero either way. A Bayesian averager over explanation sets behaves like the coherent case, so the expected sign of cross-world transfer is negative unless the two worlds share relational form — which the target system found is rarely the case (17/37 shared full forms on seed 1 was not enough to produce a gain).
- Across all 2026 negatives, the mechanism blamed is the same: the carried object encodes structure specific to the source (positional statistics, rule family, room layout) that the target does not share. None blame the *amount* of prior; all blame its *shape*.

### Gaps
- Choi & Kim 2026 (MERIT) and Chen et al. 2026 (OPR), cited by 2607.15587 as documenting negative transfer, were not opened; titles beyond the method names not confirmed.

## Key question 5: Per-paper judgement for the symbolic per-action effect predictor (does it help?)

### Takeaway
Of 20 verified papers, two are directly instructive for the target system's failed prior-transfer attempts (Hidden Rules: replicates the negative; Kostin et al.: explains it and prescribes the only safe remedy — aggregate with the empty-mind candidate, expect no gain at small margins); one offers a reusable mechanism for representation birth (IBD's per-slot two-sample test under own actions); the rest are neural/pixel-bound or assume given features and do not transfer to a no-LLM symbolic text-world learner.

### Inferences
- The literature from June–Nov 2026 contains no demonstrated positive transfer of mechanism *form* to a new environment family for any learner, neural or symbolic. The target system's three NO-GOs are in line with, not behind, the field.
- The only well-supported directions that remain open for the target system are (i) carry *cases* rather than prior summaries and let an aggregator include the empty mind as a candidate (Kostin et al.; 2607.15587), (ii) use own-action two-sample contrasts to birth attributes (IBD), (iii) prefer relative/invariant attribute encodings over absolute ones (2610.41041), and (iv) expect any transfer to be bounded by shared relational form, not by the amount of prior life (Hidden Rules).
